The atelier

Forty karigars, one floor, no outsourcing.

This page is the reason a lehenga costs what it costs. Every hour listed here is worked within a kilometre of this shop.

The work

Four techniques, named honestly.

Zardozi — metallic thread couched onto the surface with a needle, the heaviest and oldest of the four. It is what gives a bridal lehenga its weight and its shine at a distance.

Aari — chain stitch worked with a hooked needle from beneath a stretched frame. Fast in skilled hands, and the only way to fill a large ground evenly.

Dabka — coiled wire, cut and laid stitch by stitch. Used for outlines and the raised texture you can feel with a fingertip.

Gota patti & kundan — appliquéd ribbon and hand-set stones, done last, by two people who have done nothing else for thirty years.

The process

Six months, in six steps.

This is the actual sequence for a bridal commission. Everything else we make is a shorter version of it.

The first conversation

Ninety minutes, no fabric touched. We ask about the venue, the light, the time of day, what your mother is wearing, and what you have already bought. Most of it is listening.

Drawing

A hand-drawn design on paper within ten days. It is revised with you — twice, usually — before anything is cut. Nothing goes to the floor until you have signed the drawing.

Base and palette

Fabric is dyed to the drawing, not chosen off a shelf. If you are matching an heirloom, bring it in; we colour-match to the original thread under three different lights.

The frame

Fourteen to twenty weeks of embroidery. Two to six karigars on one frame, depending on density. You are welcome on the floor to see it — most brides come twice.

Fittings

Four for a bridal piece. The third is the honest one, where we make you walk, sit and lift your arms. The fourth is in the week of the wedding, after everyone's weight has moved.

Delivery and after

Delivered boxed, with a written note of what was used. We re-fit any piece we have made, free, for as long as the house exists — including for your daughter.
